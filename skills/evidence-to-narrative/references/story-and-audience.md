# Design an argument around an actual decision

## Brief and reader

Use TOSCAR to resolve material context, not to force six questions: Trouble(problem/why now), Owner(decision maker), Success(outcome and observation window), Constraints(budget/data/authority/practical limits), Actors(input/execution/affected roles), Reference(prior attempts and usable learning). Infer supplied facts; label reversible assumptions; ask only for missing information that changes consequential correctness. A useful brief identifies the choice, what success means, what can be changed and what would rule out an option. It is not complete just because a framework table has every cell filled.

Match reader, decision and data grain. Leadership may need a choice and tradeoffs, managers may need segment contributions and resource allocation, operators may need eligible cases and exceptions. These are starting points, not role stereotypes. Select the level at which the reader can change something. A company total cannot identify which orders an operator should handle; an order dump cannot explain which company-level tradeoff a leader should choose. Preserve needed drill-down and meaningful caveats for every audience. A layered artifact can serve compatible readers; genuinely different permissions or decisions can need different outputs.

Define the intended change in understanding before drafting: what the reader already knows from the brief, what the evidence adds or corrects, and which choice this enables. Treat a supposed prior belief as an assumption unless supplied. For a factual update, the change may simply be knowing the new state and whether a threshold requires attention.

## Evidence before rhetoric

Exploration considers and challenges patterns; explanation chooses what this reader needs. Maintain enough working evidence to revise the argument. Select the takeaway from evidence, then work backward to an opening, support and resolution. A familiar important risk or mixed finding can be useful; neither surprise nor monetary impact is mandatory. The main takeaway is a supported proposition, not a topic label such as "sales analysis" and not a decision already chosen before analysis.

An optional arc is Setting/Hook -> Rising evidence -> Aha/main takeaway -> Resolution. Context gives the audience a common starting point; the hook raises the question the evidence answers; each supporting step changes what the reader can conclude; the resolution follows from that conclusion. The hook can be an honest question or consequential contrast. Do not fabricate a dramatic event or delay an urgent decision for suspense. In a short memo, put the finding and recommendation first. Outline before visual polish where helpful, without producing an unnecessary planning document.

Use Eliminate -> Narrow -> Direct to test the argument: which alternative is actually ruled out, where the gap is localized, and which evidence supports the recommended direction. A segment contrast may narrow the investigation without proving cause. State uncertainty at the relevant link. Include findings in the main argument, accessible evidence, or an explicitly unresolved list according to their decision relevance; do not discard contradictory results to fit a fixed number of insights. A detail belongs in the main message if removing it changes the recommendation, confidence, scope or permitted action. Otherwise put it where the reader can verify or use it, rather than forcing an appendix page for every exploratory result.

Read the headline sequence without the body. Each headline should supply a finding or a useful question, and the sequence should connect to the decision. If a heading can be removed without changing the argument, it may be a redundant topic label. If the headings jump from a trend to a causal explanation, restore the missing evidence or weaken the explanation.

Translate Technical -> Business -> Action without changing the proposition. A coefficient remains an association unless design supports stronger inference. Financial stakes require compatible bases, periods and calculations. Retention loss does not automatically equal recoverable ARR, and advertising revenue return does not equal incremental profit ROI. Time, reliability, service quality and safety may be the relevant stakes.

## Resolution and medium

The source's 4D connects Problem -> Outcome -> Actions -> Measures. Use it to check that the recommendation addresses the stated problem and has a way to observe progress. Include a proposed or confirmed accountable role, feasible window, dependencies, tradeoffs, costs and stop/reversal rule where relevant. An outcome target is not a forecast. With sufficient evidence choose a concrete action; otherwise choose a specific diagnostic/experiment and say which result changes the decision.

Useful editing prompts are opening, context, finding, impact, proposal and next step. They are not six mandatory paragraphs. Respect the user's requested length and language, without imposing universal timing/word/slide quotas. Change packaging for the actual reading situation:

| Medium | Visible surface should do | Detail and use |
|---|---|---|
| Decision email | Subject identifies the finding or request; body states the choice, decisive evidence and needed response | Put the decision-changing qualification in the body; attach/link supporting analysis when useful; draft remains unsent without authorization |
| Operational alert | Identify affected scope, observed change or threshold, consequence and response | Give eligible cases, an accountable role and the route to current detail when needed; avoid broadcasting an executive essay |
| Live presentation | Make each slide's finding readable, with a visual comparison or evidence that supports it | Sequence slides to answer the audience's next logical question; notes/appendix carry elaboration, while interpretation-changing definitions, assumptions and contrary evidence stay visible |
| Memo/report | Work without the author's narration; put the conclusion and choice where readers begin | Show reasons, important alternative, limits and next step; keep source/calculation detail accessible for challenge or implementation |
| Shared dashboard | Let the reader locate their decision level and relevant state | Keep metric/filter/refresh meaning visible and give usable drill-down; do not substitute a fixed story for ongoing exploration |

An attachment is not a dumping ground: its labels and structure should let the reader find the promised evidence. A short channel does not excuse hiding the uncertainty that changes the action.

For every standalone artifact, test the meaning without its companion files or presenter. Name the measured unit in reader language: repeatable passages, transactions or sessions do not establish a count of unique people. Keep material observed exposure and time-bin/calendar assumptions near the comparison or operating window they govern. A PDF that omits speaker notes needs those essentials on its pages; the workbook or notes can retain the full derivation.

Treat speaker notes and footnotes as factual output. Reuse the computed facts, eligibility and denominators behind the visible evidence; calculate a reported range from the displayed groups rather than estimating it from memory. Scope assertions about absent, null or zero values to the checked field and population. Narrative compression can shorten the explanation, but must preserve the metric's meaning.

Retain the operator in reader language: a sum of transaction values can grow while the number of rows falls. “Eligible rows” names a population, not whether the claim measures their value, quantity, count or distinct IDs. Keep that measure explicit beside the percentage and comparator, including short chart titles and notes.

In a standalone decision memo, distinguish the choice now from the later outcome decision. Keep the operational eligibility and criterion governing the requested choice on the visible page: launch prerequisites, a stop trigger or an improvement boundary as applicable. Explain the main outcome's denominator in the reader's operating terms. Link the full protocol without making the reader open it to discover what they are approving or what would change the choice.

Before adding another table or framework, ask which reader action it enables. Put the preferred option, strongest relevant alternative, decisive tradeoff and condition that would change the choice close together. Separate an operational check-in from an outcome readout when their timing differs. Keep supporting reconciliations accessible without making the reader repeat the analysis to find the decision.

For a visual message, separate narrative roles from decoration. A heading states the finding; the chart shows the comparison; a nearby note qualifies it; the action text says what follows. Repeating all four as equally large colored boxes creates competing entry points. Choose emphasis according to what the reader must notice first, while retaining readable support. Use the chart specialist for visual encoding and composition rather than prescribing a branded template here.

## Working canvas

For meeting slides, compose action and source pages around the next reading task instead of placing whole paragraphs in every table cell. A short ordered sequence can show what happens now, who acts and which completed evidence permits the next step; a separate compact comparison can carry alternatives or budget amounts. Group qualifications with the relevant step and preserve decision-changing context visibly. Keep fuller field forms, calculation details and source prose in an accessible companion. Reorganize dense content without shrinking essential type or imposing a fixed slide/word quota.

Read the main action condition literally: does it require evidence already verified, or merely say that evidence needs verification? Distinguish permission to gather missing evidence from permission to launch the later intervention. Use observable completed states for the latter's prerequisites, with who checks them and the consequence of missing evidence. Do not invent eligibility rules or numeric thresholds that the brief/source cannot authorize.

For a substantial story, capture decision/candidate hypotheses, audience, stakes, honest opening, support, main finding, action, measure, risk/rollback and portable message. Fill known context and mark unknowns; do not prewrite an expected Aha as the answer. Reuse this canvas only when it helps execution, not as an eligibility gate for a small request.

## Acceptance

Check truth and use as separate questions. Material claims trace to facts, decisive figures and factual notes agree across all outputs, the strongest contradictory evidence remains visible, and recommendation/authority/measurement fit the brief. Inspect the saved editable format and each required export separately at the destination size. Compare chart categories, labels, values and essential qualifications across them; a correct export does not establish that the editable file renders identically. Use the relevant native artifact method for rendering and data/cache checks, recording any format that could not be verified.

For a reader check, show only the delivered artifact and ask for the main finding, its comparison, the qualification that matters and the requested next step. If the response misses one, inspect whether wording, evidence order, grain or visible hierarchy caused the problem and repair that part. A self-check or another model's readback can locate ambiguity; it does not establish measured human comprehension, speed, professional taste, adoption, approval or realized value.
