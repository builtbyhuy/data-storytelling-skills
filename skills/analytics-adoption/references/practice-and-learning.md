# A pilot from report to decision to observed result

## Start at the current situation

Select a real recurring question and decision owner. Inventory only relevant inputs, conflicts, metric definitions, refresh needs and data access. A one-off file analysis can proceed with a source snapshot and scoped definitions; maturity levels describe improvement opportunities, not a ban on useful work.

Cover three responsibilities: an authoritative source/snapshot for each fact; shared meanings such as grain, population, refunds, currency and period; stewardship for changes, version, freshness and tolerances. A local file and agreed dictionary may be enough. Add a governed shared dataset or semantic layer only when the practice benefits from it.

## Pilot stages

1. Context: select questions, roles, source map, definitions and decision criteria.
2. Analysis and draft: reconcile relevant facts, prioritize useful findings, draft a feasible recommendation and expose competing explanations.
3. Communication: choose the appropriate memo/dashboard/chart/deck, inspect actual output and rehearse only when oral delivery benefits.
4. Use and learn: deliver within authorization, record decisions and execution, observe results and revise the method.

Choose dates and effort from available capacity; a fixed thirty-day program, fixed insight count or quota of signed actions is not a promise. A justified stop or bounded experiment can be a valuable decision. Source stewardship, engineering, analysis, editing and independent challenge are responsibilities; they need not be five separate hires or always separate agents.

## Minimal reusable records

**Metric ownership:** metric, purpose, definition, grain/population/period/unit, source and version, owner role, refresh, exception threshold, permitted changes and approval responsibility.

**Story canvas:** decision/hypotheses, audience, stakes, opening, support, main finding, action, measure, risk/rollback and portable message. Do not require every cell before a small task; an expected finding remains a hypothesis until examined.

**Decision log:** question and evidence snapshot, proposal/options, decision/approver state, execution owner/window, outcome/guardrails, comparison and actual observed result, follow-up choice. Record unknowns explicitly. Formal identifiers and spreadsheet templates help repeated use but are optional for a short pilot.

## Review and learning

Check metric reconciliation and source conflicts, honest claim strength, relevant counterevidence, legibility and matching values, feasible action authority, actual deliverable and explicit state. Independent review is useful for consequential risk and complements direct checks. Do not score a checklist and treat it as approval or realized benefit.

Observe whether people could find the decision, whether an action occurred, which errors or delays arose, and what outcome/side effects were actually measured. Baseline comparisons use equivalent definitions and windows. Distinguish successful delivery from causally attributable business improvement. Retain only needed evidence and authorized feedback; a retrospective does not authorize storing client secrets or editing long-term memory.

Revise definitions, method or cadence after a demonstrated problem. State the next bounded change and its acceptance criteria, keep a recoverable prior version and check affected behaviors. Do not promise automatic lifelong improvement or launch background monitoring as a side effect of planning.
