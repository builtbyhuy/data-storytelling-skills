# A pilot from report to decision to observed result

## Start at the current situation

Select a real recurring question and decision owner, then state the intended change in practice: for example, a weekly readout should end in a recorded allocation choice rather than an unattended chart. Use the trouble, owner, success, constraints, actors and prior attempts to frame the pilot. Inventory only relevant inputs, conflicts, metric definitions, refresh needs and data access. A one-off file analysis can proceed with a source snapshot and scoped definitions; maturity levels describe improvement opportunities, not a ban on useful work.

Cover three responsibilities: an authoritative source/snapshot for each fact; shared meanings such as grain, population, refunds, currency and period; stewardship for changes, version, freshness and tolerances. A local file and agreed dictionary may be enough. Add a governed shared dataset or semantic layer only when the practice benefits from it.

## Pilot stages

1. Context: select the decision and actual participants; agree on source authority, metric meanings and criteria for the choice. Keep the expected finding as a hypothesis.
2. Analysis and draft: reconcile relevant facts, examine alternatives and write the evidence-to-choice argument before designing the medium. Findings that do not change this choice remain available as supporting detail.
3. Communication: produce the actual memo/dashboard/chart/deck, inspect its render and check that a reader can identify the finding, affected choice and important limit. Rehearse when oral delivery benefits.
4. Use and learn: deliver within authorization, record the decision and execution, then evaluate only outcomes whose evidence is mature. Revise the method from observed use, errors and results.

Choose dates and effort from available capacity; a fixed thirty-day program, fixed insight count or quota of signed actions is not a promise. A justified stop or bounded experiment can be a valuable decision. Source stewardship, engineering, analysis, editing and independent challenge are responsibilities; they need not be five separate hires or always separate agents.

## Make responsibilities inspectable

Name the role responsible for each consequential handoff. One person may cover several duties; independent challenge is useful when the decision's risk warrants it.

| Handoff | What is passed | Who answers for it |
|---|---|---|
| Business question → analysis | Decision, constraints, definition of success and prior attempts | Decision owner confirms the question; analyst frames what can be learned |
| Source → finding | Snapshot, metric meaning, coverage, freshness and calculation | Source steward resolves definition/access issues; analyst checks the interpretation |
| Finding → communication | Main claim, decisive support, counterevidence and limits | Analyst owns evidence integrity; editor checks the reader-facing explanation |
| Proposal → execution | Approver state, eligible scope, steps, capacity and stop rule | Decision owner approves the actual choice; execution owner implements it |
| Execution → learning | What happened, matured outcomes, harms and comparison limits | Accountable pilot role decides what to revise, continue or stop |

For an AI-assisted workflow, generated queries, drafts and checks are working products. The team's source and decision roles still carry accountability. A model review can expose a problem; it does not establish human approval or transfer responsibility to the model.

## Make the readout executable

Write each decision gate as an observable state, separating the activity from its result: a need to verify, an assigned verification, and verified evidence are different states. Authorizing a diagnostic step may be appropriate while the answer is unknown; a later intervention can be proposed or conditionally approved, while permission to launch becomes effective only when the stated evidence/prerequisites are satisfied. Name who checks them, what record establishes the state, and whether missing evidence means defer, revise or stop. Keep those conditions concise and visible in the decision handoff.

Check operational readiness against the actual proposed work. A team count, spending ceiling or approved measurement design does not by itself confirm that the assigned people, necessary equipment, partner time and access are available for the required schedule. Identify only critical resources for that proposal, the role confirming their allocation/availability and the evidence that makes the condition complete. An existing budget or measurement-plan confirmation can include this check if its meaning is explicit. “Ready to submit” remains separate from approval and permission to launch. Keep unresolved requirements as unknown/defer states without invented staffing, equipment counts or costs.

Separate the work cadence from the date when evidence can support a decision. For each decisive outcome, identify the applicable last eligible action/cohort entry, full follow-up window, metric calendar period, and source finalization delay. Derive the first usable readout from the latest required availability date. Count reporting periods using the source definition and timezone; do not replace a calendar month with four weeks. If cases continue entering, close intake or evaluate explicitly matured cohorts rather than claiming the whole pilot is complete.

An evidence-ready date does not extend an authorized pilot, budget, or fixed decision deadline. At a fixed deadline, apply the supplied decision rule or authorized fallback; report immature or unavailable evidence as not established, and label any later follow-up as a proposal.

A weekly review can still check execution, permissions, workload and early guardrails. Label its outcome judgment provisional until the required evidence is ready. At the final readout, distinguish a measured miss from an unobserved result; give missing evidence a bounded resolution date or operational stop rule rather than silently treating it as success or failure. Do not prolong a harmful action merely to finish measurement.

Choose a measure the action can still change. Rescuing an already-late case may reduce backlog age or further delay without reversing its missed original SLA. Keep the original SLA truth, and use an appropriate recovery outcome alongside it. Make the smallest useful follow-up fit the actual staff budget, including verification and logging time.

## Populate the smallest useful records

Combine these into one page or a small table when appropriate. Each field below resolves a real handoff question; it is not a requirement for a one-sentence KPI answer.

| Record | Minimum content for recurring use |
|---|---|
| Metric ownership | Metric and purpose; event/grain, eligible population, period, unit and formula; authoritative source/version; steward; refresh/finalization; exception tolerance and who can change the definition |
| Planning canvas | Decision and live alternatives; reader and stakes; proposed hypothesis; decisive evidence needed; supported main finding after analysis; action, outcome measure, risk/rollback and short portable message |
| Decision log | Question and evidence snapshot; proposal/options; actual decision and approver state; execution owner/window; eligible scope; outcome/guardrails and comparison; mature readout; observed result; follow-up choice |

Show the decision owner and metric steward separately even when one person covers both. A metric label alone cannot reconcile two departments using different refund or eligibility rules. Link the canvas to the source definition rather than retyping a slightly different meaning in each report.

An action record should answer: what changes, who has authority, who does the work, which cases are eligible, what would stop it, and what evidence permits a later judgment. A blank outcome cell means unobserved. Do not fill it with a forecast or the target.

Make review conditions observable. Identify the evidence that would cause a slot, allocation or procedure to change, the accountable role and the next permitted response. A phrase such as "adjust if needed" does not tell an operator how to act. Keep genuine unknowns as prerequisites; when the brief needs independent execution, include the needed observation form and definitions rather than requiring the author to explain them later. A discussion proposal can be shorter, but its portable summary must still distinguish the measured source, planning assumptions and unapproved action.

## Worked handoff: recovering overdue cases

This is a synthetic planning example, not evidence of a deployed team's results. Its conditions illustrate how the records work together: a support lead is considering one weekly recovery batch; twenty already-overdue cases are available; the proposed budget is four staff-hours including forty minutes of checking and logging; handling is estimated at ten minutes per case.

| Planning item | Populated proposal |
|---|---|
| Decision | Should next week's capacity include a bounded overdue-case recovery batch? |
| Scope and capacity | Up to twenty eligible cases assigned Monday 09:00. Four hours minus forty minutes for checks/logging leaves 200 handling minutes; at the estimate, twenty cases fit. Stop work at the authorized budget if handling runs longer; already-assigned unrecovered cases remain in the outcome denominator. |
| Responsibilities | Support lead is the proposed approver and accountable pilot role; an assigned operator executes; the queue steward confirms the eligible snapshot and status definitions. Assignments require the team's confirmation. |
| Metric | Recovery within two business days of batch assignment: recovered eligible cases divided by all cases assigned to that batch. Preserve each case's original SLA miss; the recovery cannot turn an overdue case into an on-time one. |
| Guardrails | Track new-case SLA misses and actual staff time. Do not divert protected new-case capacity; halt intake when the authorized time budget is exhausted. Agree on the new-case guardrail tolerance before execution. |
| Evidence and window | Record Monday's assigned cohort and each recovery event. The last eligible case needs follow-up through Wednesday 17:00; the queue export finalizes Thursday 09:00. The whole-cohort readout can start Thursday 09:00. A Tuesday review checks execution and early risk only. |
| Acceptance decision | The proposed recovery target is at least 80%, with time within four hours and the agreed new-case guardrail satisfied. The support lead confirms those criteria before execution. At the fixed Friday 17:00 decision, apply the authorized fallback if the export is unavailable; later follow-up is a separate proposal. |
| Comparison and limit | Compare with a similarly eligible historical cohort if available, reporting any differences in case mix or staffing. A before/after improvement alone would not establish the recovery batch's causal effect. |
| Current state | Proposal prepared; approval, execution and outcome unobserved. |

The resulting short reader-facing message can be: “Use a bounded recovery batch for overdue cases, with a separate check on new-case harm. Keep the original SLA misses visible and evaluate the fully followed cohort on Thursday.” The concrete choice remains with the support lead; an attractive memo cannot supply missing authority or evidence.

This record is practical because the action, capacity, outcome and readout fit together. A high dashboard count, a signed decision or an early rate from only the easiest completed cases would not satisfy the outcome criteria.

## Review and learning

Check metric reconciliation and source conflicts, honest claim strength, relevant counterevidence, legibility and matching values, feasible action authority, actual deliverable and explicit state. Independent review is useful for consequential risk and complements direct checks. Do not score a checklist and treat it as approval or realized benefit.

Observe separate links in the chain: could the intended reader identify the decision and important limit; did the authorized owner make a choice; did the execution owner implement it; and what mature outcome or side effect was measured? Use actual feedback, decisions and event records for those questions. Baseline comparisons use equivalent definitions and windows. Distinguish successful delivery, team use and beneficial business results; causal attribution needs suitable evidence beyond an ordinary before/after comparison. Retain only needed evidence and authorized feedback; a retrospective does not authorize storing client secrets or editing long-term memory.

Revise definitions, method or cadence after a demonstrated problem. State the next bounded change and its acceptance criteria, keep a recoverable prior version and check affected behaviors. Do not promise automatic lifelong improvement or launch background monitoring as a side effect of planning.
