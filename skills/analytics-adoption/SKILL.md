# Make recurring analysis useful in practice

Read [practice and learning](references/practice-and-learning.md) and relevant [evidence contract](references/evidence-contract.md). [Coverage](references/source-method-map.md) and [terminology](references/terminology.md) describe the method provenance when needed.

Start with the team's actual decisions, roles, access, reporting cadence and constraints. Design a proportionate pilot and produce usable artifacts: source/metric ownership, a selected decision question, a brief or draft report, decision log and observable learning plan. Do not invent staff availability, authority, approvals or promised outcomes.

Separate source authority, metric meaning and stewardship. Use existing files/tools when adequate; a warehouse, semantic service, paid BI tool or five-person team is not a prerequisite. Reuse available diagnosis/narrative/chart/review methods for the pilot's actual outputs. Infrastructure work occurs only if the project requires and authorizes it.

Give the pilot an accountable role, input/refresh expectations, exceptions/escalation, review responsibility and evidence of success. Record proposed, approved, executed and measured states distinctly. Choose what to revise, continue or stop from observed use and outcomes, not output counts or an internal quality score. Installation and a completed draft do not establish team adoption.

Deliver a practical plan and editable templates scaled to the brief. For a short memo with a tracking record, keep only fields needed to decide, execute and revisit; put derivations in working evidence. Reuse the same verified facts across both outputs. A direct independent check of the decisive calculations and schedule can cover the handoff: do not create another verification framework that merely repeats those passed checks or compares a generated file to its own source object. Rerun affected checks after a change or unresolved failure, then hand over the requested files.

Verify responsibility and access consistency, metric definitions and feasible cadence; use specific next steps even when names or dates remain proposed. Scheduling monitors, changing systems and sending reports need their actual authorization.
