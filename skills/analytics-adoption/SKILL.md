# Make recurring analysis useful in practice

Use the requested artifact language and locale; otherwise follow the user's language, with English as the fallback. Adapt to the actual audience, subject and medium; historical examples do not set language or scenario defaults.

For a recurring team practice or substantive pilot, read [practice and learning](references/practice-and-learning.md); it includes usable records and a worked handoff. Read the relevant [evidence contract](references/evidence-contract.md) for material facts and source conflicts. A small update to an existing tracking record can use its settled definitions and affected checks directly. [Coverage](references/source-method-map.md) and [terminology](references/terminology.md) describe the method provenance when needed.

Start with a real recurring decision and the change the team wants to make in its practice. Frame the trouble, decision owner, success and evidence window, constraints, affected roles and prior attempts. Design a proportionate pilot and populate the records with the actual case: source/metric ownership, a selected decision question, a brief or draft report, decision log and observable learning plan. Mark a proposed role or unresolved field clearly; do not substitute empty templates for a usable handoff or invent staff availability, authority, approvals or promised outcomes.

Separate source authority, metric meaning and stewardship. Use existing files/tools when adequate; a warehouse, semantic service, paid BI tool or five-person team is not a prerequisite. Reuse available diagnosis/narrative/chart/review methods for the pilot's actual outputs. Infrastructure work occurs only if the project requires and authorizes it.

Give the pilot an accountable role, input/refresh expectations, exceptions/escalation and review responsibility. Connect every proposed action to a measure it can still change, a feasible comparison, a mature readout and a stop/continue rule. Work cadence and outcome availability are different dates; a late source does not extend an authorized budget or fixed decision deadline.

Record proposed, approved, executed and measured states distinctly. Learn separately whether the report was understood, whether a decision occurred, whether the action was executed and what outcome or harm was observed. Choose what to revise, continue or stop from that evidence. Output counts, a signed decision, installation or a completed draft do not establish beneficial team adoption.

Deliver a practical plan and editable templates scaled to the brief. For a short memo with a tracking record, keep only fields needed to decide, execute and revisit; put derivations in working evidence. Reuse the same verified facts across both outputs. A direct independent check of the decisive calculations and schedule can cover the handoff: do not create another verification framework that merely repeats those passed checks or compares a generated file to its own source object. Rerun affected checks after a change or unresolved failure, then hand over the requested files.

Verify responsibility and access consistency, metric definitions and feasible cadence; use specific next steps even when names or dates remain proposed. The portable plan should give the next operator an applicable action, an observable review trigger and the record needed to apply it. If a form or operational definition is essential to the requested pilot, supply a populated example or link to the team's usable version. Keep an unrequested deployment or source update as a separate proposal. Scheduling monitors, changing systems and sending reports need their actual authorization.
